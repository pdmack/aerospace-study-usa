# MIT Financial Aid — What International Students Need to Know

---

## The short version

MIT meets 100% of demonstrated financial need for every admitted student, including international students. No loans required — aid is grants only. If you get in and have need, the money is there.

Getting in is the hard part.

---

## Undergraduate financial aid

**MIT is need-blind for US citizens** — your financial situation has no bearing on the admissions decision.

**MIT is need-aware for international students** — if it's a borderline admissions call, your financial situation can factor in. Not a disqualifier, but a consideration. Once admitted, the aid policy is identical regardless of citizenship.

**What "meets 100% of need" looks like in practice:**

| Family income (approximate) | Expected parent contribution |
|---|---|
| Under $75,000/year | $0 |
| $75,000–$140,000/year | Scaled, typically low |
| Above $140,000/year | Increases with income and assets |

Aid covers tuition (~$60,000/year), room and board (~$20,000/year), and personal expenses. The formula also considers family assets, savings, and other factors — not just income.

About 58% of MIT undergraduates receive aid. Median aid package covers most or all of cost of attendance for families with incomes under ~$140,000.

**To apply:** Submit CSS Profile and FAFSA (or international equivalent documentation). MIT requires detailed financial information from outside the US for international students — the process is more complex than for domestic students but the office is experienced with it.

---

## Graduate financial aid — different system entirely

The undergraduate aid policy does **not** apply to graduate students. Graduate funding works differently:

**PhD students** are funded through research assistantships (RA) or teaching assistantships (TA). This is employment, not financial aid — you are paid a stipend (~$40–44k/year at MIT AeroAstro) and your tuition is waived as part of the appointment. This applies regardless of citizenship.

**Master's students (SM)** are generally not funded unless a faculty member takes you on as an RA from their grant. There is no institutional financial aid for MIT master's students equivalent to the undergraduate system. An unfunded MIT SM costs ~$60,000/year in tuition.

**In short:**
- Undergraduate + need → MIT financial aid applies, significant support possible
- PhD + admission → stipend and tuition waiver, no separate aid needed
- Master's alone → typically self-funded or RA-dependent, very expensive without funding

---

## The realistic picture

MIT undergraduate admits roughly 3–4% of applicants — one of the lowest acceptance rates in the world. For international students who need significant aid, the need-aware policy adds a further consideration.

The financial aid is genuine and generous **if you are admitted**. The admissions gauntlet is the real constraint, not the aid itself.

If the goal is a US graduate degree in aerospace engineering, a PhD program (fully funded) or a TU Delft master's (EU tuition, near-zero cost) are more financially straightforward paths than MIT undergraduate followed by graduate study.

# Financial Aid at Elite US Universities — What You'd Actually Pay

The sticker price at MIT, Harvard, Princeton, and Stanford is not what most students pay. These universities have the largest endowments in the world and use them to make attendance affordable for admitted students regardless of where they're from, including Denmark.

---

## The key policy

MIT, Harvard, Princeton, Stanford, Columbia, and a handful of other top US universities meet **100% of demonstrated financial need** for every student they admit — including international students. No loans. Grants only.

What this means in practice: the amount you pay is based on your family's financial situation, not the university's published price.

| Family income (approximate) | What you'd likely pay |
|---|---|
| Under $75,000/year | Little to nothing |
| $75,000–$140,000/year | A modest, income-scaled contribution |
| Above $140,000/year | Increases gradually with income and assets |

The scary tuition numbers you see online (~$60,000/year) are what families with high incomes and significant assets pay. For most international students from professional but not wealthy families, the actual cost is substantially lower.

Princeton goes furthest — it was the first to eliminate loans entirely. Every dollar of aid is a grant you don't repay.

---

## One important nuance

These schools are **need-aware** for international students at the undergraduate level. For domestic US students they are need-blind — your finances play no role in the admissions decision. For international students, if it's a close admissions call, financial need can factor in. Once admitted, the aid is fully met.

This is not a reason to avoid applying. It is just worth knowing.

---

## Graduate programs — a completely different system

For PhD programs, financial aid doesn't enter the picture at all. PhD students are funded through **research assistantships (RA) or teaching assistantships (TA)** — you are employed by the university, paid a stipend ($40–50k/year at top programs), and your tuition is waived entirely. This applies to international students the same as everyone else. You do not pay to do a PhD at MIT, Harvard, Princeton, or Stanford. You are paid.

The cost question only applies to:
- Undergraduate admission
- Standalone master's programs (which are typically unfunded — see the master's section in programs.md)

For anyone considering a PhD, the cost of attendance is effectively zero. Focus the financial concern on the master's decision, not the PhD.

---

## The bottom line

If you are admitted to MIT, Harvard, Princeton, or Stanford for undergraduate study, you will almost certainly pay far less than the published price — possibly nothing, depending on your family's finances. The endowments exist precisely so that money is not what determines who can attend.

The obstacle is admission, not cost.

For PhD programs: you will be funded. Full stop.
